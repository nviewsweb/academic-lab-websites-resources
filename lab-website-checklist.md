# Research Lab Website Checklist

**40 practical checks to make your research easier to find, understand, and maintain.**

A research lab website is more than a list of publications. It is a public home for your research identity, people, discoveries, recruitment opportunities, and contact information. This free checklist helps professors, principal investigators (PIs), postdoctoral researchers, and lab administrators review an existing website or plan a new one.

> **Guiding principle:** Built for people. Structured for machines. Managed for researchers.

**How to use it:** Mark each applicable item as complete. If an item does not apply (for example, recruitment when you are not hiring), mark it **N/A** rather than treating it as a failure. Review the highest-impact items first. This is a practical self-assessment, not a legal, accessibility, or security certification.

## 1. Lab identity and credibility

- [ ] **1. Clear identity:** The lab's full name, university or organization, department, and research field are easy to find.
- [ ] **2. PI and affiliations:** The PI's name and current affiliation are accurate; link relevant official profiles where appropriate.
- [ ] **3. Stable address:** Use a memorable domain or durable institution-hosted URL; document who controls it and its renewal.
- [ ] **4. Authentic information:** Research claims, achievements, funding acknowledgments, and contact details are current and verifiable.

## 2. Homepage and navigation

- [ ] **5. Immediate explanation:** A first-time visitor can understand what the lab studies in a few seconds.
- [ ] **6. Clear paths:** Navigation leads to Research, Publications, People, Opportunities (if relevant), and Contact.
- [ ] **7. Mobile-friendly layout:** Text, menus, images, and forms work on a phone without horizontal scrolling.
- [ ] **8. Useful next step:** Visitors can easily contact the lab, explore recent work, or find open positions.

## 3. Research projects and publications

- [ ] **9. Research themes:** Main research areas have concise, jargon-aware explanations and useful links.
- [ ] **10. Updated publication records:** Titles, authors, journal/conference, year, and DOI or persistent link are accurate.
- [ ] **11. Discoverable publication pages:** Where individual paper pages are used, each has a stable URL and appropriate bibliographic metadata.
- [ ] **12. Lawful access to full text:** Link to publisher/open-access versions; share accepted manuscripts or personal copies only when permitted by the relevant policy.

## 4. People, alumni, and recruitment

- [ ] **13. Current team:** Profiles show members' names, roles, and research interests with permission.
- [ ] **14. Alumni and transitions:** Departed members are moved to an alumni section rather than left as current staff.
- [ ] **15. Recruitment status:** Open opportunities specify role, eligibility, application method, deadline, and supervisor/contact.
- [ ] **16. Applicant privacy:** If collecting CVs or applications, provide an appropriate privacy notice, restricted access, and a defined retention process.

## 5. Search engine discoverability (SEO)

- [ ] **17. Descriptive page titles:** Every important page has a clear, distinct title and useful meta description.
- [ ] **18. Crawlable pages:** Important public pages are not accidentally blocked by `robots.txt`, `noindex`, or authentication.
- [ ] **19. Sitemap and canonical URLs:** Publish a working XML sitemap where appropriate, and use consistent canonical URLs to avoid duplicates.
- [ ] **20. Working internal and external links:** Fix broken links, old university-server URLs, dead DOIs, and incorrect redirects.

## 6. Machine-readable research identity and AI discovery

- [ ] **21. Structured data:** Use relevant Schema.org types (for example, `Organization`, `Person`, `ScholarlyArticle`, or `Dataset`) when they truthfully describe the page.
- [ ] **22. Persistent identifiers:** Connect ORCID, DOI, ROR, dataset repositories, and other authoritative identifiers where available.
- [ ] **23. Consistent names and relationships:** Keep lab, institution, PI, project, and publication names consistent across the website and external profiles.
- [ ] **24. Public, understandable content:** Use descriptive headings, accessible HTML, concise research summaries, and links to supporting sources; do not rely on images alone to communicate important facts.

**Important:** Structured data and AI-readable pages can help machines interpret research information, but **do not guarantee** indexing, search rankings, appearance in AI answers, or citations by AI systems. `llms.txt` is optional and not a replacement for accessible content, standard metadata, or an XML sitemap.

## 7. Accessibility and performance

- [ ] **25. Readable design:** Maintain sufficient text contrast and comfortable text sizing.
- [ ] **26. Keyboard usability:** Navigation, links, menus, and forms can be used without a mouse.
- [ ] **27. Accessible content:** Use meaningful headings, descriptive link text, appropriate image alt text, and captions/transcripts where needed.
- [ ] **28. Page speed:** Optimize images, scripts, and fonts; test representative pages on mobile and slower connections.

## 8. Security and privacy

- [ ] **29. HTTPS everywhere:** The site uses valid HTTPS and does not load insecure resources.
- [ ] **30. Controlled access:** Only authorized people can edit or administer the site; use multifactor authentication where supported.
- [ ] **31. Backups and recovery:** Backups are scheduled where needed, and someone knows how to restore the website after an incident.
- [ ] **32. Privacy-by-design:** Collect only necessary personal data, check third-party embeds and analytics, and assess applicable privacy/consent requirements.

## 9. Maintenance and continuity

- [ ] **33. Named website owner:** A person or team is responsible for content and technical changes.
- [ ] **34. Update routine:** Define when to review publications, team members, vacancies, news, and funding acknowledgments.
- [ ] **35. Monitoring:** Check uptime, broken links, forms, key downloads, and relevant technical errors on a practical schedule.
- [ ] **36. Exit and migration plan:** Keep a record of domain ownership, hosting access, content exports, and redirect needs if the lab moves institutions or providers.

## 10. Outcomes and research visibility

- [ ] **37. Useful measurement:** Track privacy-conscious aggregate metrics such as popular pages, publication interest, and relevant inbound links, if appropriate.
- [ ] **38. Working contact routes:** Test contact forms or email addresses and specify how collaboration inquiries should be made.
- [ ] **39. Shareable research outputs:** Where applicable, connect publications with datasets, code, protocols, project pages, and appropriate licenses.
- [ ] **40. Regular review:** Revisit this checklist at least every six months and after major changes in the lab.

## Quick self-assessment

Count **only applicable checks**.

| Result | Interpretation | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 90–100% complete | Strong foundations | Maintain the update routine and review specialist needs |
| 70–89% complete | Good starting point | Prioritize gaps affecting visitors and discoverability |
| 50–69% complete | Several important gaps | Plan an organized update, starting with core identity, links, and access |
| Below 50% complete | Substantial work may be needed | Consider a structured rebuild or migration plan |

These bands are **informal guidance**, not validated benchmarks or an external quality certification.

### The five checks to tackle first

1. **Can visitors understand the lab's research and find the right contact?**
2. **Are the PI, team, publications, and open positions accurate?**
3. **Does the site work on mobile and over HTTPS?**
4. **Can search engines reach public pages, and do links work?**
5. **Is someone responsible for updates, backups, and domain access?**

## Suggested review schedule

| Frequency | Activities |
| --- | --- |
| On each new publication or staffing change | Update publication records, member profiles, and related research pages |
| Monthly | Check key forms, important links, and current recruitment details |
| Quarterly | Review accessibility issues, analytics trends, research highlights, and technical health |
| Every six months | Re-run this checklist, check ownership and access, and review backups and migration readiness |
